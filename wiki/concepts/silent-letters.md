# Silent letters

**One line:** many letters at the end of French words are written but not said.

## Explanation
Final consonants are usually silent, final -e is usually silent, and the verb ending -ent is
silent. Some final consonants *are* pronounced, so beginners need rules of thumb plus memory.
A silent final consonant can "wake up" before a vowel (see liaison).

## Key points
- Final consonants are often silent. [S1 §Silent letters and word endings] «A final written consonant is often silent»
- But some are pronounced. [S1 §Silent letters and word endings] «But some are pronounced: avec /avɛk/, sac /sak/»
- The verb ending -ent is silent. [S1 §Silent letters and word endings] «The verb ending -ent is silent in ils parlent»
- Final -e can make the consonant before it heard. [S1 §Silent letters and word endings] «it can make the preceding consonant audible: petit/petite»
- Final c, r, f, l are usually pronounced (CaReFuL). [S2 §What letters should or shouldn't be pronounced?] «If a word ends with consonants c, r, f, l»
- Other final consonants are not. [S2 §What letters should or shouldn't be pronounced?] «If a word ends with any other consonant»
- Final -e is silent unless it's the only vowel. [S2 §What letters should or shouldn't be pronounced?] «Except when the -e is the only vowel sound in the word»
- Many silent letters come from Latin-based respellings. [S5 §French orthography] «further increased the number of silent letters»

## Examples
- `petit` ≈ puh-TEE /pəti/ vs `petite` ≈ puh-TEET /pətit/

## Disagreements
- **CaReFuL vs -er words.** S2 says final c, r, f, l are pronounced [S2 §What letters should or shouldn't be pronounced?],
  but its own table shows -er endings like *parler* said as /e/ with no r [S2 §Combinations with "e" at the end of a word].
  Treat CaReFuL as a rule of thumb with exceptions; S1 says to learn each word's sound [S1 §Silent letters and word endings].
