# Schwa (e muet, the "mute e")

**One line:** the weak "uh" vowel spelled e, which often disappears in speech.

## Explanation
Many unaccented e's in French are a short, relaxed "uh" sound called schwa. Very often it isn't
pronounced at all, especially in the middle of words and at the end of words. So a word can
have fewer spoken syllables than written ones.

## Key points
- Its other names are e caduc and e muet. [S3 §Schwa] «also called e caduc ('dropped e') and e muet ('mute e')»
- It's "unstable": often dropped. [S3 §Schwa] «The main characteristic of French schwa is its "instability"»
- Often dropped after one consonant mid-word. [S3 §Schwa] «That is usually the case when it follows a single consonant in a medial syllable»

## Examples
- 🔊 `appeler` ≈ ap-LAY (the middle e drops) /aple/
