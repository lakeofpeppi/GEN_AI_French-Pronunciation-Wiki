# The French r

**One line:** a soft, throaty r made at the back of the mouth, not with the tongue tip.

## Explanation
The usual French r /ʁ/ is made where the back of the tongue meets the soft palate, close to
where you gargle. You don't curl the tongue like an English r. Being understood matters more
than a perfect accent.

## Key points
- Made at the back of the mouth. [S1 §The French r] «is made toward the back of the mouth»
- Clarity beats copying an accent. [S1 §The French r] «Clarity matters more than imitating a particular regional accent»
- Start from an English h and tighten the throat. [S2 §Hey, what about the French "r"?] «tightening your throat a little more on the “h.”»
- Any r is understandable. [S2 §Hey, what about the French "r"?] «any “r” will do»
- It's usually uvular, and varies by region. [S4 §Notes] «The French rhotic /ʁ/ is usually guttural (uvular), but it varies by region»

## Examples
- `rue` ≈ rü (throaty r) /ʁy/
