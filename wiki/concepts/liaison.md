# Liaison

**One line:** a normally silent final consonant is pronounced when the next word starts with a vowel.

## Explanation
French links words together. If a word ends in a silent consonant and the next word starts
with a vowel sound, that consonant is often pronounced and glued onto the next word. The s/x
usually comes out as a z sound. Some liaisons are required, some optional, some forbidden.

## Key points
- A silent final consonant becomes audible before a vowel. [S1 §Liaison and intonation] «a normally silent final consonant becomes audible»
- s and x usually sound like z. [S1 §2. Three liaison sounds to practice] «s and x usually sound like [z]»
- Never link after et. [S1 §6. Do not connect every final consonant] «Never make a liaison after et»
- Some H words block liaison. [S1 §4. What about words beginning with H?] «Other H words block a liaison»
- Linguists call it a "latent" final consonant. [S3 §Liaison] «having a "latent" final consonant that is pronounced only in certain syntactic contexts»
- Mandatory: determiner + noun. [S6 §Mandatory liaison] «between a determiner and a following adjective or noun»
- Mandatory: pronoun + verb. [S6 §Mandatory liaison] «between a subject or object pronoun and the verb»
- Optional ones sound formal. [S6 §Optional liaison] «The realization of optional liaisons is a signal of formal register»
- Impossible after et. [S6 §Impossible liaison] «after certain words, for example et ("and"), and all singular nouns»

## Examples
- `les amis` ≈ lay-za-MEE /lezami/

## Disagreements
- **Should a beginner make optional liaisons?** S6 says pedagogical grammars often recommend making as many
  as possible in careful speech, while informal speakers skip some to avoid sounding stilted [S6 §Optional liaison].
  S1 just says to follow the recording [S1 §6. Do not connect every final consonant]. Deck advice: always make the
  mandatory ones, don't worry about optional ones yet.
