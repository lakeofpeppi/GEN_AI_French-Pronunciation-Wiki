# Accents and marks

**One line:** marks on letters that often change the sound (é, è, ç) and sometimes don't (ê, ô).

## Explanation
French uses accents and other marks on letters. Some tell you how to pronounce the vowel, the
cedilla changes c into an s sound, and some marks only distinguish words in writing.

## Key points
- é usually means /e/. [S1 §Accents and other marks] «commonly marks /e/: été»
- A cedilla makes c sound like s. [S1 §Accents and other marks] «A cedilla makes c sound /s/»
- The circumflex doesn't always change the sound. [S1 §Accents and other marks] «it does not always change the sound»
- é represents /e/. [S5 §Diacritics] «An acute accent over ⟨e⟩ represents /e/»
- à vs a and où vs ou only tell words apart. [S5 §Diacritics] «is primarily used to distinguish homophones»
- A circumflex often marks a lost s, as in fête from feste. [S5 §Diacritics] «it indicates the historical deletion of an adjacent letter»
- The cedilla turns c into /s/. [S5 §Diacritics] «A cedilla under ⟨c⟩ indicates that it is pronounced /s/ rather than /k/»

## Examples
- `été` ≈ ay-TAY /ete/ · `garçon` ≈ gar-SOHN /ɡaʁsɔ̃/
