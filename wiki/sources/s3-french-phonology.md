# S3 — French phonology (Wikipedia)

## What it covers
The linguistic description of French sounds: the vowel and consonant inventory, nasal vowels
and their mergers, schwa (e muet), liaison, elision, stress and intonation.

## Key points
- Up to 13 oral and 4 nasal vowels. [S3 §Vowels] «Standard French contrasts up to 13 oral vowels and up to 4 nasal vowels»
- Many speakers, especially around Paris, use only one of the un/in pair. [S3 §Nasal vowels] «many speakers use only the latter phoneme, especially most speakers in northern France such as Paris»
- Liaison: a "latent" final consonant appears before a vowel. [S3 §Liaison] «having a "latent" final consonant that is pronounced only in certain syntactic contexts»
- Schwa (e muet) often isn't pronounced. [S3 §Schwa] «The main characteristic of French schwa is its "instability"»
- Stress never changes a word's meaning. [S3 §Stress] «it has always been agreed that word stress is not distinctive in French»
