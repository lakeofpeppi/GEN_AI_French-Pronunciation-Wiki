# S1 — Prononciation — Les bases du français 101 (open textbook)

## What it covers
A beginner chapter from *Introduction to French Level I* (CC BY 4.0): silent letters, oral vs
nasal vowels, open/closed vowels, the French r, accents, liaison, enchaînement and intonation,
with practice exercises.

## Key points
- Spelling and sound don't match letter by letter. [S1 §Prononciation — Les bases] «French spelling and pronunciation do not correspond letter by letter»
- Final consonants are often silent, but some are pronounced. [S1 §Silent letters and word endings] «A final written consonant is often silent»
- The verb ending -ent is silent. [S1 §Silent letters and word endings] «The verb ending -ent is silent in ils parlent»
- Nasal vowels send some air through the nose. [S1 §Oral and nasal vowels] «For a nasal vowel, some air also flows through the nose»
- Many speakers merge two of the nasal vowels. [S1 §Oral and nasal vowels] «Many speakers merge the last two sounds»
- The French r is made at the back of the mouth. [S1 §The French r] «is made toward the back of the mouth»
- Liaison makes a silent final consonant audible before a vowel. [S1 §Liaison and intonation] «a normally silent final consonant becomes audible»
- Never link after et. [S1 §6. Do not connect every final consonant] «Never make a liaison after et»
