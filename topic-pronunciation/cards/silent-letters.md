# card: silent-letters   (copied from wiki/concepts/silent-letters.md, this node may only see that page)
- claim: Final consonants are often silent.
  cite: S1 §Silent letters and word endings
  quote: A final written consonant is often silent
- claim: But some are pronounced.
  cite: S1 §Silent letters and word endings
  quote: But some are pronounced: avec /avɛk/, sac /sak/
- claim: The verb ending -ent is silent.
  cite: S1 §Silent letters and word endings
  quote: The verb ending -ent is silent in ils parlent
- claim: Final -e can make the consonant before it heard.
  cite: S1 §Silent letters and word endings
  quote: it can make the preceding consonant audible: petit/petite
- claim: Final c, r, f, l are usually pronounced (CaReFuL).
  cite: S2 §What letters should or shouldn't be pronounced?
  quote: If a word ends with consonants c, r, f, l
- claim: Other final consonants are not.
  cite: S2 §What letters should or shouldn't be pronounced?
  quote: If a word ends with any other consonant
- claim: Final -e is silent unless it's the only vowel.
  cite: S2 §What letters should or shouldn't be pronounced?
  quote: Except when the -e is the only vowel sound in the word
- claim: Many silent letters come from Latin-based respellings.
  cite: S5 §French orthography
  quote: further increased the number of silent letters
